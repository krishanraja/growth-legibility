# Copy doctrine for Legibility builder outreach

Status: Current. Written 19 October 2026 from the Copywriting, Offers and Demand Corpus (`_corpus/copywriting-offers-demand-corpus.md`, received 2026-10-19, reference only). Section names below are the corpus's own.

This is how every draft, distribution asset and review aid this engine prepares should think. It is guidance for the people and agents who write the exact text that `src/stages/prepare.mjs` checks. It is not execution authority, it does not change policy, contracts or the approval path, and where it disagrees with AGENTS.md, Control Center canon or Krish, they win.

It serves the North Star directly: 75 builders with a first trusted read and 30 returning, each earned by a useful product test or a rich named invitation, never a list.

## The one rule

The corpus closes on: identify a valuable change people already want, build a credible and differentiated way to achieve it, explain it in the form they are ready to understand, and measure whether they act and succeed. For Legibility that reads: a builder whose agent needs product data it can rely on, shown on their own products what Legibility can and cannot read, then measured by a first trusted read and a return, never by a reply.

## The market: high sophistication, proof hungry

Builders of agentic buying, procurement, comparison and commerce infrastructure (the playbook's proposed ICP; the current approved ICP comes only from Control Center) have heard every "product data API" claim. In Schwartz's terms (Breakthrough Advertising, Sophistication) this is a late-stage market: naked claims are discounted, so lead with a **mechanism** and a **demonstration**.

- Mechanism: per-field calibrated confidence, a 0.7 trust gate, honest coverage that reports what failed and why, one typed schema over REST and MCP, response-level cost.
- Demonstration: the builder's own products run through the test. Hopkins (Scientific Advertising, "Offer service before demanding action") and the proof hierarchy below both put this first.
- Never ask copy to rescue weak demand (Breakthrough Advertising, Operational rules). If the approved ICP or a current signal is missing, the engine holds; do not write around the hold.

## Persuasion chain: find the weakest link first

Use the nine links (Part I, The persuasion chain) to diagnose before rewriting. The usual Legibility failure points:

| Link | Legibility question | Asset that answers it |
|---|---|---|
| Relevance | Is this builder in the approved ICP with a current, cited signal? | Approved ICP, `current_signal.url` |
| Attention | Does the opening name their project, not ours? | Cited observation |
| Difference | Why this over scraping or another data vendor? | Mechanism, honest coverage |
| Belief | Will it read my products? | Their own test result, never our assertion |
| Value | Is a first trusted read worth the integration? | Value Equation below |
| Safety | What if my retailers are not covered? | The test says so instead of guessing |
| Timing | Why now? | A real trigger in their work, or none |
| Action | What exactly do I do? | One low-friction step: run the test |

## Awareness and lead type by touch

Label awareness before choosing a lead (Great Leads, Operational rules; Part IV, Phase 7). Use the most direct lead the reader can accept.

| Touch | Typical awareness | Lead | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Named cold builder, approved ICP, current public signal | Problem aware, product unaware | Problem-Solution or Secret (the mechanism) | Name company and project (`cold_draft_not_specific` otherwise). No warmth claims |
| Warm builder with cited relationship evidence | Solution or product aware | Promise, only with the proof attached | Relationship URL must be cited; no invented familiarity |
| Introducer or multiplier | Their audience's problem | Problem-Solution for their readers | One ask; publication or forwarding stays theirs |
| Returning builder (first trusted read done) | Most aware | Offer: the next concrete step | One human-reviewed follow-up at most |

Avoid Proclamation and Story for cold builders: they spend attention before relevance, and Proclamation slides toward the banned hype patterns. A Promise lead is never used without the test or a sourced result beside it.

Rule of One (Great Leads): one reader, one idea, one promise, one action per draft. The one action is almost always "run the test on your own products".

## USP test

Run Reeves' three gates (Reality in Advertising, The USP test) on any proposition before it enters a draft. Working form:

> For a builder whose agent buys or compares products, Legibility returns product data with per-field calibrated confidence and a trust gate, so the agent knows what it can rely on, because the test shows what resolved, what was trusted and what failed on the builder's own products.

- Proposition: a concrete benefit (the agent can rely on what it reads), not an image.
- Uniqueness: the playbook's combination of calibrated confidence, response-level cost, MCP access and confidence-gated billing. Competitors may claim any one; do not claim "unique", show the combination.
- Selling force: only if the builder's own failure mode is unreliable product data. If it is not, the candidate is a poor fit, not a copy problem.

## Feature, benefit, proof

Translate every feature (The Copywriter's Handbook, Benefit translation) and keep the qualification visible.

| Feature | Consequence | Builder benefit | Evidence | Qualification |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Per-field calibrated confidence | Agent can gate on each field | Fewer wrong buys or comparisons | Their test result; app README | Calibration is per stratum; never merged silently |
| 0.7 trust gate | Below-gate reads are flagged | Agent knows when not to act | Test report | Below-gate is not a trusted read |
| Honest coverage report | Failures named with reasons | No silent bad data | Test report | Strongest on structured pages, Shopify and barcodes today |
| REST and MCP, one typed schema | One integration path | Less integration effort | App README | Key follows verification |
| x402 path | Agents could pay per call | Future autonomous purchase | App source | Base Sepolia testnet only |

## Value Equation for a first trusted read

Use Hormozi's levers (The $100M Offers, Value Equation) as a design check, not a pricing formula.

- **Dream outcome:** the builder's agent reads their own products and can trust the result. Specific, identity relevant, testable.
- **Perceived likelihood:** raise it with honest coverage, the 0.7 gate and their own result. Never with "works everywhere" (`universal_coverage` is a hard draft failure).
- **Time delay:** bring the first meaningful result forward: the free test before any key or integration. Do not state a duration that no source supports.
- **Effort and sacrifice:** one typed call over REST or MCP, up to five products in the test. Do not promise effort savings the builder has not seen.

Bonuses, guarantees and price are offer changes. They are Krish's and the product's decisions, surfaced as `wall: strategy` proposals, never written into a draft first.

## Proof hierarchy and proof cards

Match proof to claim (Part IV, Phase 6). For Legibility, in order of strength:

1. The builder's own test or API result (direct observed outcome under stated conditions).
2. A live demonstration on a public product with the conditions stated.
3. A consented case study from a comparable builder, owned by the source owner.
4. Consented benchmark aggregates with sample sizes and method disclosed.
5. Process proof: the mechanism, the gate, the coverage statement.
6. Unsupported assertion: never in a draft.

Testimonials and logos require source-owned consent and are representative of nothing beyond themselves. Every factual claim in a draft carries a proof card's fields in the draft's `source_refs` and research notes: claim, source URL, method, context, limitation, approved wording, review date. The source register's revisions and 17 November 2026 review date apply to product claims; a stale source means the claim waits.

**Testnet versus mainnet.** x402 runs on Base Sepolia. Say "testnet". Any "live on mainnet" wording fails as `mainnet_claim`, and Sepolia volume is never revenue. Only reviewed live evidence, recorded in the source register, changes this.

## Ethical influence

Use one Cialdini principle (Influence) that resolves the live doubt, never a stack:

- Reciprocity: the free test report is genuine value given first.
- Authority: the mechanism and method, not titles.
- Social proof: consented, comparable builders only.
- Scarcity and urgency: none unless real and confirmed by Krish. "Founding 200" is a target, not a deadline or a stock limit.
- Commitment: invite a reversible step (run the test), never a trap.

## Test record, mapped to experiments

Keep one active material acquisition experiment. Record it with the corpus's test record fields (Part V, Test record), with these meanings:

- `primary_metric`: first trusted reads or mature seven-day returns by source, never replies or opens.
- `guardrails`: suppression honoured, trust rate, no paid media, maximum five prepared items per run.
- `result`: measured only from source-owned complete coverage. Unknown stays unknown; partial coverage reads `inconclusive`.
- `context`: synthetic runs, fixtures and demo output never count and are never a control.
- Respect the minimum-sample tripwires before any `adopt_challenger`. Test market and offer before wording (Part IV, Phase 12).

## Before drafting

- Is there a current approved Legibility ICP from Control Center, and is this builder in it?
- What current, cited signal makes this relevant now?
- What awareness level, and therefore which lead?
- What one result matters to this builder, and what proof of it exists?
- Is the relationship claim, if any, cited?
- What one exact action, and is it the test?

## Before handoff to Control Center

Run the edit passes (Part IV, Phase 9) in order: A strategy (one reader, promise, action), B evidence (every claim cited, limitations visible), C flow (each sentence earns the next; Sugarman), D clarity (familiar words, no unexplained jargon), E action (one exact step, real reason or none, easy opt-out). Then confirm the engine's own checks will pass: signal and relationship sources cited, no placeholders, no banned claims, company and project named for cold drafts. Return assumptions and evidence gaps separately from the draft (Part VII, Drafting instruction).

## Rubric, adapted

Score 0 to 4 on the corpus's twelve dimensions (Part VI, Evaluation Rubric) as a review aid. It is not a wire field and changes no approval:

Audience (approved ICP and trigger), Desire (their stated or observed need), Awareness fit (lead matches the touch table), Differentiation (mechanism, not "unique"), Offer value (the test lowers delay and effort), Proof (claim-matched, cited, qualified), Clarity, Flow, Objections (coverage and integration doubts answered where they arise), Ethics (no invented warmth, urgency or proof), Action (one exact step), Measurement (outcome traced by source to first trusted read).

Use the corpus bands. A 0 on Proof or Ethics holds the draft whatever the total. A high score proves only that the draft is worth Krish's review, not that it will work.

## Where this engine's rules change the corpus

1. No sender, publisher, list buyer or payment executor. The corpus's distribution and follow-up advice ends at a prepared draft; Krish acts.
2. Named drafts only, for builders in a current approved Legibility ICP from Control Center. No generic sequences, purchased or scraped lists, or the corpus's seven-email sequence. One message and at most one human-reviewed follow-up.
3. No invented urgency, scarcity, guarantees, testimonials, statistics, customer language or warmth. The corpus allows real versions; here even real ones need source evidence or Krish's confirmation.
4. No mainnet or universal-coverage claims without reviewed live evidence. x402 is testnet.
5. Synthetic runs, fixtures and demo output never count as live achievement or as test results.
6. Price, bonuses, guarantees and offer changes are Krish and product decisions, surfaced as proposals, never drafted first.
7. Control Center owns approvals, the queue, ICP and source completeness. A rubric score or a passing check is not approval.
8. Zero paid media. The corpus's paid channel is closed.
9. Voice-of-customer and case material uses only source-owned, consented records. Never commit real people, drafts or private research; fixtures stay synthetic.
10. No em dashes in new material.
